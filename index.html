        Check it with your lead line

          How to take a good check
          • Pick a charted spot depth on a flat, even bottom, away from drop-offs, so being a boat-length off the spot doesn't matter.
          • Stop the boat. Lower the lead until the line goes slack, then lift until it just touches, with the line straight up and down.
          • Read the mark at the water surface, not at the deck or the gunwale.
          • Take three casts and enter the middle one. Soft mud and weed let the lead sink, so a firm bottom gives a truer reading.
          • Choose a calm spell: wind piles water up at the downwind end of a lake.
          • Check the rope's marks against a tape now and then. Rope stretches and shrinks when it's wet.

            Official reports

              Waterways Ireland's report lists each lock's ordinary summer level and the 9:00 reading, published by 9:30 on weekdays (weekend readings appear on Monday). Its levels and the ESB's are in Poolbeg datum: take off 2.71 m to compare them with this page (at Banagher, take off 2.91 m: Waterways Ireland's Banagher figures run 20 cm above OPW's on the same gauge). Ardnacrusha uses about 100 m³/s per turbine, so around 400 means all four are running and Lough Derg is being drawn hard.

                Data

                • Office of Public Works (OPW): water levels from the gauges at Portumna, Banagher, Shannonbridge, Athlone, Hodson's Bay and Carrick-on-Shannon, from waterlevel.ie, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Readings are provisional, as the OPW publish them. This page compares them with reference levels and works out depth corrections from them; those workings are ours, not the OPW's.
                • Met Éireann: the inland lakes forecast and the HARMONIE model point forecast. Copyright Met Éireann. Source www.met.ie. This data is published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence. Met Éireann does not accept any liability whatsoever for any error or omission in the data, their availability, or for any loss or damage arising from their use. This page has modified the data: it shortens the written forecast, picks out the lakes on this stretch, and converts and summarises the model's wind and rain.
                • Waterways Ireland: marine notices, lock opening hours, ordinary summer levels, lock cill heights and the daily navigation levels report. The marine notices shown are short summaries written by an AI assistant, with a link to each official notice on waterwaysireland.org, which is the authority.
                • ESB: links to its Shannon flow and level reports on esbhydro.ie.
                • CSIG: the chart datum figures behind the depth correction come from the CSIG's published articles and forum posts. The depth correction is meant for use with CSIG charts. No CSIG chart data is shown or shared on this page.
                • IWAI Athlone branch: the Lough Ree chart datum and the lake's record low used to set it, and the method and bridge height behind the Athlone M6 bridge air draft.
                • shannon-river.com: the distance chart behind the cruising-time estimates.
                • Lough Ree Yacht Club and venue newsletters: events on the water.
                • The venues themselves: menus, opening hours and phone numbers, from their own websites. The star ratings are an experimental estimate written by the AI assistant from public review scores and awards. They don't come from the venues, and no venue has seen or approved them.
                • Sunrise and sunset times are worked out by the page itself.
